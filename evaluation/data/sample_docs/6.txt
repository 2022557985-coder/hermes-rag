Paracetamol (acetaminophen) is a common analgesic and antipyretic medication. The standard adult oral dose is 500-1000 mg every 4-6 hours, with a maximum daily dose of 4000 mg for healthy adults. Overdose can cause severe liver damage. Ibuprofen is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) that also has analgesic and antipyretic effects. Its typical adult dose is 200-400 mg every 4-6 hours, with a maximum daily dose of 1200 mg for over-the-counter use. Unlike paracetamol, ibuprofen has anti-inflammatory properties and can cause gastrointestinal irritation. Aspirin, or acetylsalicylic acid, was first synthesized by Felix Hoffmann at Bayer in 1897. In addition to pain relief, low-dose aspirin (75-100 mg daily) is commonly used for cardiovascular prevention.
